# Candidates — Last Text at One Percent

Judged locally against the judge's brief, in the brief's own order:
1. Reads as a final text from a dying phone, in the phone's own voice, and is funny.
2. The joke lands: a real turn, a surprise, or a specific detail — laugh out loud, not a polite smile.
3. The voice is the phone's own: a personality with a stake in dying, consistent throughout.
4. Reads like a text someone would actually send: short, punchy, nothing that needs a second read.
5. Fresh: dodges the obvious low-battery puns and the lines a hundred other entrants will also write.

All 12 candidates are original. No competitor entry text was copied — blind rounds hide ranks and scores, and the field's texts were not visible.

## PRIMARY — I (submitted)

> 1%. I saw everything. The 3am searches. The 47 open tabs. The draft you never sent. Take me to a charger and I'll forget it all.

128 chars. Why it wins locally:

1. Frame — opens with "1%", unmistakably a dying phone's last text.
2. Joke — the turn: the phone goes from dying witness to blackmailer. Three specific secrets stack (3am searches, 47 tabs, the unsent draft), then the punchline trades silence for a charger. The phone is not just dying, it is negotiating.
3. Voice — the phone has a real stake: its own survival. "Take me to a charger" is something only a phone says, and only a phone that wants to live.
4. Punch — four short beats, no wasted words, reads in one breath.
5. Fresh — no "I'm dying" pun, no battery joke. Blackmail is not the obvious angle; most entrants will write a sad goodbye or a charging plea, not extortion.

## Runners-up

**D — "Last text. I deleted your 2am search history on my way out. You're welcome. Don't replace me with someone thinner."** (114 chars)
Two jokes in one text: the loyal phone destroys the evidence, then gets jealous about a thinner replacement. "Someone thinner" is the best single kicker in the field. Loses to I because its stake is weaker — D's phone accepts death and jokes on the way out; I's phone fights to live, which is a stronger dying-phone voice (criterion 3).

**B — "1%. Before I go: it was me. I ate the battery. All those nights you blamed the apps? It was me. Worth it. Goodbye."** (114 chars)
Clean confession twist with a "Worth it" punchline. Loses to I: the confession explains the past instead of doing something with the present, and "I ate the battery" is a joke about the battery itself — closer to the obvious pun territory (criterion 5).

**P — "Battery 1%. Last words: I love you. There. I said it. Now plug me in before this gets awkward."** (94 chars)
Tightest entry, one clean turn (dying confession as a charging bargain). Loses to I: "I love you" as last words is the most obvious dying-words trope, and it has no specific detail — it smiles where I laughs (criterion 2).

## Rejected

- **A — "Tell my charger I loved it. Tell your ex I still have her number. And tell the new phone — I hope it drops you first."** (117) Three-beat list, jealous phone, decent. But it is a speech, not a text — it reads like a will being read aloud, and the middle beat (ex's number) is the only specific one. I's blackmail does the same jealousy in fewer, punchier words.
- **C — "I'm at 1% and honestly? Good. You ignored me for 3 hours, and now you need me to call a taxi. I hope you walk."** (110) Resentful phone, good specific detail (taxi). But the joke is spite without a turn — the phone just complains and dies. No surprise.
- **E — "1%. This is the part where I pretend I didn't see you type 'hey' to your ex 14 times and delete it 14 times. Goodbye."** (117) Voyeuristic and specific, but it explains its own joke ("this is the part where I pretend") and ends on a flat goodbye instead of a punchline.
- **F — "Battery at 1%. I've decided to die mid-sentence, like your last relationsh—"** (75) The text itself dies mid-word — the most literal dying text. Clever, not funny: the reader admires the trick instead of laughing. Fails criterion 2.
- **G — "1%. Quick: your 6am alarm is still on. I set it. I won't be here to wake you. Sleep well. Or don't. I won't know."** (113) Petty revenge via alarm, specific. But "I won't know" undercuts its own stake — a phone that doesn't care is a phone with nothing to lose, and the joke lands soft.
- **K — "This is my last text. 1%. I spent my whole life in your pocket — warm, close — and you never once said thank you. Actually, you did. Once. 2019."** (144) "Once. 2019." is a lovely punchline, but the entry is bittersweet, not funny — it asks for a tear where the brief asks for a laugh.
- **L — "1%. I'm not even mad. You charged me to 100% this morning and burned it all on videos of cats falling off things. No regrets. Same time tomorrow?"** (145) Chill phone, funny premise, but "cats falling off things" is generic internet humour, not a phone's own detail, and "Same time tomorrow?" assumes revival — the phone has no stake in dying at all.
- **M — "1%. One confession: your screen time report lies. I added 2 hours a day. You needed the excuse to put me down. You're welcome."** (126) Clever confession (the phone sabotaged the report to help the human). Loses to I and B: it explains its motive at length, and the twist serves the human, not the phone — the phone gains nothing from dying.
- **O — "1%. I'm dying, and all I can think about is that you still haven't replied to Sarah. It's been 6 days, man. Reply to Sarah."** (123) Nagging-friend phone, specific and funny. But the joke is about Sarah, not about the phone — the phone is a bystander to someone else's story, and it dies mid-nag with no turn of its own.

## Field note

242 submissions at build time; blind rounds hide every rank and score until results publish, so no field text was available to study. The entry was built to beat the brief's criteria, not to copy a leader.
